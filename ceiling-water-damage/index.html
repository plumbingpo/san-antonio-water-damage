Ceiling Water Damage Repair Help in San Antonio

The stain overhead comes at the end of the story, not the beginning. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

Signs to Look For

  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
  • Small black or green specks appearing in a cluster overhead
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Lights and Wiring

Can lights, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are located exactly where the water is. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Older Ceiling Materials

Some older popcorn textures and plasters contain asbestos. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Mold Above You

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Risk of Collapse

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Make It Safe

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
