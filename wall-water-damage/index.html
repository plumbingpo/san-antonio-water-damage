Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What San Antonio Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Wet tack strip, lifting planks or stained flooring at the base of the wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Growth Behind the Paint

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Water Next Door

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.
