Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in San Antonio, San Antonio Restoration Co is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Porous materials that contacted sewage, including carpet, padding, drywall, insulation, mattresses and upholstered furniture, have to be discarded. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. If the panel and thermostat can be reached without contact with sewage, shut off electricity to that area and stop the air system. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
Sewage backups are extremely dangerous. Hepatitis, respiratory disease and E. coli infection are among the serious illnesses caused by parasites, bacteria and viruses found in raw sewage.
