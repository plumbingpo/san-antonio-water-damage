AC Leak Water Damage in San Antonio: What to Know

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanPress towels into damp carpet, mop hard flooring, put a bucket below any drip from the ceiling, and clear furniture, clothes and boxes out of the closet or away from the stain.
  5. Call Both TradesCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Closet Walls and Platform

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Nearby Flooring

Carpet pad, laminate and hardwood near the closet all get wet from underneath as the leak spreads past the door. Laminate swells and will not recover, and hardwood cups if it stays wet.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • The number of days water kept dripping before the AC was turned off.
  • The area of drywall and floor covering that cannot be saved.
  • Mold growth, if any, and the containment and removal it calls for.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.

Our Process

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Extract and Open Up

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
